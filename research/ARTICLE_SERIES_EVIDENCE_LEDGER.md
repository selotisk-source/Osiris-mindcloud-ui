# Article-series evidence ledger — Varna, Sofia and Cyprus

**Research pass:** 2026-10-28  
**Purpose:** Record source-backed claims, useful connections, uncertainties and next investigations for the article series. This is a research ledger, not a final archaeological report.

## Method

- Prefer primary sources, museum excavation records, government heritage agencies and UNESCO.
- Keep the source's claim separate from our interpretation of it.
- A similarity between sites is a comparative lead, not proof of direct contact, shared origin or a causal relationship.
- Record uncertainty and missing evidence instead of filling gaps with a compelling narrative.
- Personal recollections and photographs are first-hand material but must be catalogued separately from published archaeological evidence.

## Findings and research leads

### 1. Sofia: Saint Sophia basilica and the East Serdica necropolis

**Source:** Regional History Museum — Sofia, “St. Sofia” Basilica Archaeological Level  
URL: https://sofiahistorymuseum.bg/en/chain-offices/%E2%80%9Cst-sofia%E2%80%9D-basilica-archeological-level

**Source-supported claims**
- The archaeological level under the present basilica contains burial structures from the eastern necropolis of ancient Serdica and remains of three earlier churches.
- The museum describes approximately 50 exposed graves/tombs dated broadly to the 3rd–5th centuries CE.
- Burial types include stone sarcophagi, cist graves and brick-built tombs; some vaulted tombs have painted decoration.
- The museum identifies the Tomb of Honorius as a notable decorated tomb with a Christian inscription.

**Separate record:** Tomb of Honorius  
URL: https://sofiahistorymuseum.bg/en/19-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F/121-tomb-of-honorius-1989%2C-2002

The museum reports a vaulted brick tomb with painted Latin inscriptions, including “HONORIUS SERVUS CHRISTI”, and a broad date range from the third quarter of the 4th century to the end of the 5th / beginning of the 6th century. The exact interpretation and dating should be cited from the museum rather than paraphrased as certainty beyond its stated range.

**Open questions**
- Which exact tomb or sarcophagus appears in the user's photographs? Do not identify it from proximity alone.
- Does a photograph show a label, inventory number, inscription, tomb plan or spatial marker that can be matched to a museum record?
- Which visible object is original in situ, and which is a reconstruction, display or later interpretation?

### 2. Sofia: archaeological investigation at the Tsar Samuel monument

**Source:** Regional History Museum — Sofia, “Archaeological investigations at the sculptural monument of Tsar Samuel”  
URL: https://sofiahistorymuseum.bg/bg/19-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F/175-%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D1%81%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%BF%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%BD%D1%83%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D1%86%D0%B0%D1%80-%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BC%D1%83%D0%B8%D0%BB

**Source-supported claims**
- The museum describes a small excavation in late 2014 in the garden area in front of Saint Sophia, intended for the monument pedestal.
- Eleven medieval burial features were found in the 13 m² excavation area and are broadly dated to the 11th–12th centuries based on grave goods from some burials.
- The same area contained an earlier structural feature dated to the 4th–5th centuries, with a later modification dated to the 6th century.

**Important limit**
This source documents archaeological features at the monument site; it does not establish that the excavated medieval burials belonged to Tsar Samuel. Do not conflate this excavation with the earlier necropolis beneath the basilica or identify any sarcophagus as Samuel's without direct evidence.

**Open questions**
- Locate a plan of the excavation area relative to the monument and the basilica.
- Find the full excavation publication, inventory, photographs and any later scholarly discussion.
- Establish what the user remembers seeing: location, display label, inscription, exact object and whether the attribution came from a guide, label or personal interpretation.

### 3. Varna: Chalcolithic Necropolis and long-distance exchange

**Source:** Official Varna tourism portal, “The oldest processed gold treasure in the world”  
URL: https://visit.varna.bg/en/varna_oldest_gold_treasure_in_the_world.html

**Source-supported claims**
- The Varna Chalcolithic Necropolis is dated by the portal to approximately 4600–4200 BCE.
- The portal reports 294 graves found, including metal objects, pottery, flint, obsidian and shells.
- It discusses possible trade relations with distant regions and notes Mediterranean Spondylus shells among the finds.
- The portal states that roughly 30% of the estimated necropolis area remains unexcavated.

**Research lead**
Investigate the provenances of copper, obsidian and Spondylus, and compare the strength of evidence for each proposed exchange route. A material's presence can support contact or movement, but the route, mechanism and social meaning require separate evidence.

**Open questions**
- Find excavation publications and peer-reviewed archaeometric studies on metal, shell and stone provenance.
- Distinguish established material sourcing from older or more speculative interpretations of trade routes.
- Avoid calling a single route “proven” unless the relevant study directly supports it.

### 4. Paphos: Tombs of the Kings and Hellenistic Mediterranean connections

**Sources**
- Cyprus Department of Antiquities: https://www.culture.gov.cy/dmculture/da/da.nsf/All/238de8d409bf6077c225719b0039f785?Click=&OpenDocument=
- Visit Cyprus: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/tombs-of-the-kings/
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/79

**Source-supported claims**
- The necropolis was built during the Hellenistic period and continued to be used during the Roman period.
- The name “Tombs of the Kings” is not proof that kings were buried there; official sources describe burials associated with high-ranking officials and aristocratic families.
- Peristyle courtyards and rock-cut tomb architecture have parallels with Hellenistic sites including Alexandria.

**Research lead**
Compare architectural features, construction techniques, dated grave goods and documented political context. Similar architecture supports a historical comparison and possible cultural influence; it does not alone prove that a specific person travelled between the places or that every tomb had the same function.

**New publication lead**
Cyprus's Deputy Ministry of Culture announced a first final excavation volume on Tombs of the Kings (seasons 1977–1981) in May 2026:
https://www.gov.cy/en/culture/release-of-the-volume-i-tombs-of-the-kings-the-ptolemaic-necropolis-of-pafos-excavation-seasons-1977-1981/

The announcement discusses Tomb 8, possible Ptolemaic associations and architectural parallels. These are research claims from the publication announcement; the full volume and its arguments should be consulted before using the royal attribution as fact.

### 5. Gateway Process: a separate methodological comparison

**Primary document:** *Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process*, dated 9 June 1983  
CIA Reading Room: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5.pdf  
Government Publishing Office catalogue: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D101-PURL-gpo152943

**What is established**
- A 1983 U.S. Army report exists and discusses the Gateway Experience using models drawn from Bentov, quantum mechanics and theories of consciousness.
- The report's existence and government archival status do not establish that its speculative claims about consciousness, holograms or access to information beyond ordinary time are scientifically confirmed.

**How it may connect to the article series**
Use it as a comparative lens for how people build models from observations, symbols and perceived patterns. Do not use it as archaeological evidence for Varna, Sofia or Paphos. Any cross-link should be explicitly methodological, philosophical or narrative unless independent evidence supports a stronger claim.

## Candidate comparative questions

1. **Burial architecture:** Which similarities between Sofia's Late Antique tombs and Paphos's Hellenistic/Roman tombs are chronological parallels, and which have a demonstrable line of transmission?
2. **Movement of materials:** What do Varna's shells, obsidian and metal sources tell us about actual exchange networks, and what remains uncertain?
3. **Water, routes and settlement:** How did coastlines, ports, roads and local topography shape the three places in their own periods? Do not assume a single continuous network across millennia.
4. **Sacred reuse and memory:** How did later religious or civic traditions reinterpret older places? Establish the chronology of each case before proposing a pattern.
5. **Personal observation as a research lead:** Can the user's photographs, notes and recollections be matched to published records? Record exact matches and unresolved differences.
